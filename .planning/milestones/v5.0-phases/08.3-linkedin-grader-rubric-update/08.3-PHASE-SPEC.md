# Phase 08.3 — LinkedIn Grader Rubric Update (Operator-Provided Specification)

> Verbatim requirements provided at phase insertion (2026-07-09). This is the
> authoritative spec for planning Phase 08.3. Consumed by /gsd:plan-phase.

## Objective

Redesign the LinkedIn Profile Grader so that its scores accurately reflect whether the visible profile evidence helps a candidate appear credible, relevant, searchable, and compelling to recruiters.

The grader must evaluate real profile quality rather than merely checking whether fields are populated. It must also remain robust to LinkedIn PDF export formatting, especially multi-column layouts and export-generated labels.

All existing LinkedIn grading functionality should remain operational except where changes are explicitly required by this phase.

## Core requirements

### 1. Keep exactly four scoring sections

The grader must always return exactly these four sections, in this order:

- Headline — 15 points
- About — 15 points
- Experience — 45 points
- Search Alignment, Skills & Credentials — 25 points

The maximum possible score is 100.

The model must not emit an overall score. The server must calculate the total by summing the applicable section scores.

Do not add a fifth section or emit optional, reduced, or tier-dependent section sets.

## Scoring rubric

Every section must use explicit discrete scoring bands.

The model must:

- Quote or clearly identify the profile evidence used.
- Select the single band that best matches the evidence.
- Emit an integer score within that band.
- Never average toward a middle score.
- Never inflate scores for encouragement.
- Never punish the candidate for information that is not present in the PDF export and is not represented by one of the four scoring sections.

### Headline — 15 points

Evaluate whether the headline immediately communicates the candidate's professional identity, target direction, relevant specialization, and credible differentiators.

- 0–2: Missing, unintelligible, or contains no professional positioning.
- 3–5: Primarily a school, employer, or generic title with little specialization.
- 6–9: Identifies a role or field and includes some relevant terminology, but remains broad, fragmented, or weakly differentiated.
- 10–12: Clearly communicates a target role or professional direction with relevant role, domain, or specialty terminology.
- 13–15: Highly focused, credible, differentiated, and strongly aligned with the candidate's experience, skills, and apparent target opportunities.

Do not require a rigid headline formula.

Do not assume every strong headline must list multiple technologies, contain a value proposition, or include a seniority label.

Penalize keyword stuffing, unsupported claims, contradictory targets, or positioning that is not corroborated elsewhere in the profile.

### About — 15 points

Evaluate whether the About content communicates a coherent professional story, relevant capabilities, credible evidence, and career direction.

- 0–2: Missing or effectively empty.
- 3–5: Generic aspirations or personal qualities with little role context, evidence, or relevant terminology.
- 6–9: Communicates a professional direction and some relevant skills or experience, but remains vague, uneven, or thin on proof.
- 10–12: Clear and focused narrative with relevant terminology and concrete evidence of capability, contribution, or domain knowledge.
- 13–15: Compelling, concise, credible, and well-aligned narrative that connects professional identity, evidence, specialization, and direction.

Do not require multiple paragraphs.

Do not require a call-to-action.

Do not require quantified achievements when another form of strong evidence is present.

Treat the PDF export heading Summary as LinkedIn's About content. Never recommend that the user rename Summary to About, because that label may be generated by LinkedIn's export format rather than selected by the user.

### Experience — 45 points

Evaluate the quality, relevance, specificity, progression, ownership, scope, and demonstrated impact of the candidate's experience.

- 0–8: No meaningful experience evidence, or roles contain titles with little or no description.
- 9–17: Some responsibilities or activities are described, but evidence is generic, repetitive, incomplete, or weakly connected to a target direction.
- 18–27: Several concrete contributions, methods, deliverables, or outcomes are present, though quality and specificity are inconsistent.
- 28–36: Most relevant roles contain clear contributions, credible evidence, strong verbs, role-specific detail, and meaningful outcomes or scope.
- 37–45: Consistently strong, highly specific, achievement-oriented evidence demonstrating substantial ownership, complexity, results, leadership, progression, research depth, or domain authority.

Metrics are one form of evidence, not a universal requirement.

Strong evidence may include:

- Quantified outcomes
- Completed implementations
- Research methods or findings
- Technical complexity
- Product or operational scope
- Stakeholder responsibility
- Publications or adopted recommendations
- Revenue, cost, efficiency, quality, or user impact
- Leadership, founding, hiring, mentoring, or community-building
- Clear progression in responsibility

Do not require every bullet to include a metric.

Do not automatically penalize students or early-career candidates for lacking promotions, people management, or long tenure.

Do not treat all non-technical experience as weak when it supports the candidate's stated target.

Evaluate incomplete roles honestly. A title and dates alone should not receive credit equivalent to a role containing concrete evidence.

### Search Alignment, Skills & Credentials — 25 points

Evaluate whether the profile's visible titles, Top Skills, domain language, education, certifications, honors, publications, and other credentials collectively reinforce a coherent target.

- 0–5: Little usable search or credential evidence, or the visible information substantially conflicts with the candidate's positioning.
- 6–10: Some relevant skills, education, or credentials are visible, but they are generic, sparse, weakly supported, or poorly aligned.
- 11–15: A credible set of relevant skills and credentials is present, with moderate consistency across the profile.
- 16–20: Strong alignment among titles, skills, education, domain terminology, and target direction, with most important claims corroborated.
- 21–25: Exceptional consistency and depth across role titles, skills, credentials, domain evidence, and professional positioning.

This section must consider visible Top Skills as substantive evidence.

Education must be evaluated inside this section rather than as a separate fifth section.

Relevant certifications, honors, awards, publications, languages, academic work, and activities may strengthen this section when they meaningfully support the candidate's target.

Do not reward credentials merely for existing. Evaluate relevance and alignment.

Do not penalize experienced candidates solely for lacking extensive education details when their experience provides stronger evidence.

Do not assume that a skill listed in the sidebar was used in a specific job unless the experience text supports that connection.

## PDF parsing and evidence integrity

LinkedIn PDF exports may contain multiple visual columns. The parsing and grading flow must preserve section boundaries and reading order.

Implement safeguards that prevent sidebar content from being concatenated into the main profile narrative.

For example:

- Top Skills
- individual skill names
- languages
- certifications
- contact information
- honors
- publications

must not be merged into the Headline, Summary/About, or Experience text merely because PDF text extraction returns them nearby.

The grader must distinguish among:

- User-authored profile text
- LinkedIn-generated section headings
- Export metadata
- Sidebar fields
- Main-column content

The word Summary in a PDF export must map internally to the About scoring section without becoming part of the quoted About text.

Contact information, page numbers, mail links, LinkedIn URLs, and PDF footer artifacts must not influence the score.

Evidence quotations must reflect the source text accurately and must not reproduce text created by a column-order parsing error.

## Date and duration handling

- Do not invent an evaluation date.
- Do not recalculate a LinkedIn-generated duration using an assumed current month.
- Do not flag a role as internally inconsistent merely because its exported duration appears unexpected.
- Only flag a date issue when the visible dates explicitly contradict each other or are impossible based on reliable, available evidence.
- Never produce recommendations such as changing an eight-month duration to three months unless the actual evaluation date and underlying employment dates make that conclusion certain.
- Treat Present as an ongoing role unless the profile explicitly indicates otherwise.

## Hallucination prevention

All analysis and suggested copy must remain grounded in the profile.

Never invent:

- Technologies
- Frameworks
- Programming languages
- Team structures
- Collaborators
- Leadership responsibilities
- Employers
- Projects
- Metrics
- Results
- Certifications
- Coursework
- GPA
- Honors
- Career goals
- Job targets
- Dates
- Role status
- User counts
- Revenue or cost impact

A recommendation may reorganize, clarify, shorten, or strengthen facts that already exist, but it must not add unsupported facts.

For example, do not add Node.js because a profile mentions React.

Do not add phrases such as "in collaboration with the backend team" unless collaboration with that team is present in the source.

Do not recommend fabricated coursework, clubs, leadership positions, or honors.

When the available evidence is insufficient to produce a truthful paste-ready replacement, do not include that issue as a topFix. Place a non-fabricated recommendation in the relevant section's improvements array instead.

## Recruiter-search claims

Evaluate recruiter discoverability through observable alignment among:

- Job titles
- Skills
- Role terminology
- Domain terminology
- Experience descriptions
- Education and credentials
- The candidate's apparent target direction

Do not claim access to LinkedIn's private ranking algorithm.

Do not make unsupported statements such as:

- The headline is always prioritized over every other section.
- A specific keyword will cause the profile to appear in a precise number of additional searches.
- A rewrite will create a specific percentage increase in recruiter visibility.
- Recruiters will contact the user a certain number of times per week.
- A particular phrase guarantees Boolean-search visibility.
- Keywords must appear within an invented character threshold.

Expected impact descriptions must remain qualitative and defensible, such as:

- Makes the candidate's target role easier to identify.
- Improves consistency between the headline and experience.
- Gives recruiters stronger evidence of a listed skill.
- Reduces ambiguity about the candidate's professional direction.
- Makes the profile easier to scan and evaluate.

## Applicability behavior

All four sections must always be emitted.

Set `applicable: false` only when explicit profile evidence establishes that the entire combined section is legitimately irrelevant.

Missing content is not the same as inapplicability.

A missing but relevant Headline, About, Experience, or Search Alignment section must remain applicable and receive an appropriately low score.

Because education is included within Search Alignment, Skills & Credentials, a lack of formal education must not make that entire section inapplicable.

## Output contract

Preserve the existing top-level response structure unless a schema migration is required for the new section names and maximums.

Return raw JSON only.

The response must contain:

- scoreJustification
- nextBestAction
- sectionBreakdown
- topFixes
- keyStrengths

The sectionBreakdown array must contain exactly four objects, in the required order.

Each section object must contain:

- name
- applicable
- maxScore
- score
- evidence
- strengths
- weaknesses
- improvements

Required maximums:

- Headline: 15
- About: 15
- Experience: 45
- Search Alignment, Skills & Credentials: 25

Every score must be an integer between zero and the section's maximum.

Do not emit:

- overallScore
- totalScore
- rawScore
- A fifth section
- Hidden bonus points
- Negative points
- Scores above the declared maximum

The server must calculate the total as the sum of applicable section scores without a second normalization step.

## Top fixes

Return approximately four to seven top fixes when enough evidence-supported improvements exist, and never more than seven.

Rank fixes by likely profile impact.

Every top fix must contain:

- title
- currentText
- suggestedFix
- whyItWorks
- pointsGain
- priority

The currentText must quote the profile accurately or state Missing.

The suggestedFix must be immediately usable and must contain no placeholder tokens.

The suggestedFix must use only facts supported by the profile.

The pointsGain must:

- Name the affected section
- State a realistic potential gain
- Never exceed the section's remaining available points
- Avoid claiming guaranteed total-score outcomes
- Avoid awarding points for changes unrelated to the rubric

Do not create multiple fixes that claim the same overlapping points.

## Score explanations

The overall justification must identify the most important evidence affecting the score without referring to unsupported platform behavior.

Strengths and weaknesses must be specific to the profile.

Avoid repetitive commentary across:

- scoreJustification
- nextBestAction
- Section weaknesses
- Section improvements
- topFixes

The output should prioritize actionable insight rather than repeating the same observation in multiple fields.

## Frontend and server integration

Update all server-side constants, schema validators, frontend labels, progress calculations, and section-rendering logic that depend on the old maximums or section names.

Ensure the UI renders exactly four sections.

Audit any score category labels against the direct 100-point scale so that no legacy normalization remains.

Preserve the rule that the server owns total-score calculation.

Do not introduce a second percentage conversion when the four section maximums already total 100.

Ensure existing saved grader results are handled safely. Add a migration, versioned response contract, or backward-compatible renderer if old four-section results use the previous names and maximums.

The final implementation should produce differentiated scores across sparse, average, strong, and exceptional profiles while remaining deterministic, evidence-based, career-stage-aware, and resistant to PDF parsing errors and fabricated recommendations.
