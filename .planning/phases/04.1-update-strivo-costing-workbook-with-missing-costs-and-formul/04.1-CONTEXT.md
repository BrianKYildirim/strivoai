<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Shared versus scenario inputs

- **D-01:** Add a fourth worksheet named **`Sources & Inputs`**. It is the single editable source for global vendor prices, fixed subscriptions, risk rates, source metadata, included allowances, overage prices, and activation statuses. The three scenario sheets remain the user-facing operating model.
- **D-02:** Only demand assumptions vary by scenario: MAU, tier mix, feature usage, Merit-shop purchase behavior, and other volume drivers. Refund rate, chargeback rate, failed-payment rate, OCR incidence, retry overhead, vendor prices, and fixed costs are global and must not vary between Worst, Mid, and Best Case.
- **D-03:** Source-backed prices use an **actual-over-official fallback**. Keep separate `Actual Paid` and `Official Price` inputs; formulas use actual billing when present and visibly fall back to official pricing when it is absent. The active source type must be obvious.
- **D-04:** Use compact source IDs beside model inputs and a full audit table on `Sources & Inputs`. The audit table records at least item, source ID, source type, plan, value, units, included limit, overage rate, full URL/reference, pricing or billing date, last verified date, status, and notes.

### Payment-loss waterfall

- **D-05:** Use a sequential waterfall: gross billings -> unrecovered failed payments -> collected revenue -> refunds -> chargeback revenue loss and chargeback fees. Do not calculate all loss rates independently from the same gross base.
- **D-06:** Calculate subscription and Merit-shop payment economics separately using the same global loss-rate assumptions, then sum them. The workbook must expose each channel's failed-payment loss, refunds, chargebacks, Stripe processing fees, and net collected revenue.
- **D-07:** Treat annual Elite subscriptions as steady-state monthly economics: amortize annual revenue, original Stripe processing fees, failed-payment loss, refunds, and chargeback economics over 12 months. Annual subscribers still incur AI usage every month.
- **D-08:** A refund removes revenue but does not reverse the original Stripe processing fee. A chargeback removes the disputed revenue and adds the sourced dispute fee. Current official Stripe terms are the default; authoritative account-specific evidence controls if it proves different terms.

### OCR provider costing

- **D-09:** Keep the main OCR model simple by using a **blended cost per OCR page**, not provider-level calculation rows. The source audit must still document every provider underlying the blend and the real fallback order.
- **D-10:** Maintain separate Free and Elite blended OCR page rates because the allowed fallback paths differ and Claude OCR is tier-gated.
- **D-11:** Track resume and LinkedIn documents separately. Each has its own uploads, OCR incidence, and average OCR pages; those drivers then use the appropriate Free or Elite blended page rate.
- **D-12:** When production incidence or page data is unavailable, use conservative, non-zero starting estimates. Mark them visibly as estimates and list them in the production-data To-Do for replacement. A missing measurement must never silently become a zero-cost assumption.

### Optional-service treatment

- **D-13:** Keep code-confirmed services that are inactive, unconfigured, or included at no extra charge as visible `$0` rows. Each row must say `Inactive`, `Not configured`, or `Included` and explain why its modeled cost is zero. Do not invent a paid plan or cost.
- **D-14:** Optional services use a controlled status/include input. Activating a row makes its existing formulas use the actual-over-official price and applicable usage drivers; activation must not require rewriting formulas.
- **D-15:** For services included in a base plan, record the sourced allowance and calculate only usage above that threshold as an overage. Cost remains zero while usage stays within the included limit.
- **D-16:** Include one Claude Code Pro subscription and one ChatGPT Plus subscription under **Development Tools** as fixed operating costs. They reduce company profit before the existing two-founder split and tax estimate.

### the agent's Discretion

- Exact placement, row spacing, input highlighting, section boundaries, and source-table layout, provided the existing workbook's colors, fonts, borders, formats, alignment, and three-scenario visual language are preserved.
- The exact conservative OCR incidence/page estimates, refund rate, chargeback rate, failed-payment rate, and retry overhead selected during research, provided each is evidence-backed where possible and plainly marked as an estimate otherwise.
- The exact safe toggle mechanism and validation formulas for optional services, provided inactive rows cannot add cost accidentally and active rows flow through without formula edits.
- Spreadsheet tooling and recalculation/verification mechanics, provided planning identifies them before workbook edits and proves formula integrity, cached-value refresh, format preservation, and cross-scenario parity.

</decisions>

### Reusable Assets

- `docs/Strivo AI Final Costing Plan.xlsx`: contains exactly three visible worksheets (`Worst Case`, `Mid Case`, `Best Case`). Each currently has 3,108 serialized cells and 151 formulas; the formula pattern hashes identically across all three sheets. Preserve this parity while adding the fourth shared-input sheet.
- `.planning/phases/02-ai-token-cost-optimization/02-MEASUREMENT-REPORT.md`: provides measured production AI costs by feature and tier, avoiding speculative token-cost inputs.
- `docs/ENVIRONMENT.md`: provides the bounded provider inventory needed to distinguish active, optional, retired, and test-only services without guessing.
- `supabase/functions/_shared/document-extraction.ts`: provides the real provider ordering that the blended OCR rates must represent.

### Established Patterns

- The existing workbook places Overview in columns A:I, Merit Shop calculations in L:T, and AI Costing in W:AK. New sections should extend this visual language instead of reorganizing the workbook wholesale.
- Worst, Mid, and Best Case already use identical formula patterns. Scenario differences belong in inputs, not formula structure.
- The codebase treats provider configuration fail-closed or optional through explicit environment variables; a code reference alone is not proof of a paid invoice.
- Phase 02 established durable per-call telemetry and proved that actual output consumption, not `maxTokens`, drives Anthropic spend.
- The repo has no checked-in Excel authoring library or existing costing-workbook script. Planning must select and validate appropriate spreadsheet tooling before any in-place edit.

### Integration Points

- Shared global assumptions and source metadata land on `Sources & Inputs`; all three scenario sheets reference those cells while retaining local demand drivers.
- Payment-loss rows feed subscription revenue, Merit-shop revenue, Stripe fees, total monthly costs, profit, and founder take-home.
- OCR rows consume scenario document volumes plus global incidence/page assumptions and tier-specific blended page rates.
- Supabase, Vercel, Namecheap/domain, Resend, Firecrawl, OCR providers, Stripe, Anthropic, Vercel analytics, and development subscriptions flow into fixed or usage-based totals according to their audited status.

<specifics>
## Specific Ideas

- The shared sheet should make fallback state visible: an auditor must be able to tell whether a formula is using `Actual Paid` or `Official Price` without tracing the formula manually.
- Use short source IDs such as `STRIPE-01` in working rows and keep full URLs/dates in the audit table.
- Demand-only scenario variation is deliberate: global risk-rate variation was rejected so scenarios remain focused on customer volume and usage rather than stacking optimistic/pessimistic loss assumptions.
- The OCR calculation should remain simple on the scenario sheets even though its audit trail documents the complete parser -> Mistral -> Textract -> Claude OCR path.
- Visible `$0` rows distinguish a real included/inactive dependency from an omitted or forgotten cost.

</specifics>
